How to spot a X-Wing

Understand Sudoku X-Wing: use two rows or columns with matching candidate positions to eliminate possibilities.

When to look for it

  1. Select one digit and inspect its candidate positions.
  2. Find two distinct rows where the digit appears in exactly the same two columns, or reverse rows and columns.
  3. Eliminate that digit from other cells in the two matching columns, outside the paired rows.

A simple example

Suppose candidate 5 appears in rows 2 and 8 only in columns 3 and 7. It must occupy opposite corners, so other cells in columns 3 and 7 cannot contain 5.

Why it works: Each base row must contain the digit once. Their shared two-column restriction occupies both cover columns, which rules out every other occurrence there.

Common mistake

Four arbitrary candidate corners do not establish an X-Wing. Each base row must have exactly the two matching candidate positions.
